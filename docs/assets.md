## Master portraits

Found images, neither generated nor drawn: all 40 files (ten masters, four expressions each) are existing pictures downloaded from the web and only cropped, scaled and re-encoded. Nine masters are Google's Emoji Kitchen stickers, each the master's emoji combined with a face emoji: slightly smiling face for neutral, grinning face for happy, star-struck for power, crying face for sad. No emoji set has a gopher, so the Gopher is Maria Letta's drawing of the Go gopher from her Free Gophers Pack, in the same four moods. Why this cast, and which original is which, is in `docs/art/masters-sources.md`. No original game art was used, traced or copied.

The Emoji Kitchen image URLs come from the sticker index behind emojikitchen.dev (`app/metadata.json` in [xsalazar/emoji-kitchen-backend](https://github.com/xsalazar/emoji-kitchen-backend)); no license is stated where the stickers are served. Teggle is private, so licenses are listed where a source states one but decide nothing. The originals are kept unchanged in `reference/teggle-art/found-masters/` (not committed), and `sources.json` there records each one's URL, fetch date and SHA-256.

Processing, the same for every file, all by `npx tsx tools/master-art.ts` with no hand retouching. The originals already have transparent backgrounds, so nothing is keyed. Framing is measured on the master's neutral original, as the box around its pixels with alpha above 4%:

- **head:** the box's longer side becomes `fill` times the launcher window crop (0.76 of the square), and the point at `focus` of its height (0.5 unless listed) lands on the window center (x 0.5, y 0.45). The same scale and offset apply to the master's four expressions, whose originals share one canvas.
- **bust** (the Gopher, a standing figure): the figure is scaled to `height` of the square with its top at `top`, centered on its upper half, and cut off at the bottom edge, which leaves head and shoulders. Each expression is measured on its own and gets the neutral's scale.

Then a Lanczos resize onto a transparent 640 x 640 square (no scale is above 1; the stickers are 534 px) and WebP quality 90 with lossless alpha (cwebp, method 6, sharp YUV). Together the 40 files are 955,846 bytes.

## Backgrounds

Found images, neither generated nor drawn: every level's picture is an existing photo or illustration downloaded from Pexels (44), Pixabay (6) or wallhaven (5), then cropped, softened and graded in code into its stage's look and encoded as WebP. No original game art was used, traced or copied. Teggle is private, so licenses are listed where the source states one but decide nothing. A picture's full record is `background.picture` in `campaign/levels/<id>.json`; the downloaded originals stay in `reference/teggle-art/<source>/originals/` (not committed), and processing always starts from the bytes whose SHA-256 the record holds. The pictures generated earlier with a paid image API are gone.

Processing, the same steps for every file, all by `npm run campaign:art -- import <id> <ref>` and `npm run campaign:art -- process` (`tools/campaign/look.ts`) with ImageMagick 7.1.2-33 Q16-HDRI and cwebp 1.6.0, no hand retouching: the crop at the shipped aspect, Lanczos to half size, softening (`paint`: a selective blur of radius 2 x paint at 12% contrast, then Kuwahara at radius paint; 1 for a painting and 2 for a photo unless the level sets it), Lanczos to 2732 x 1644, a Gaussian blur of 1.2 x paint, brightness and saturation, the stage palette's gradient map blended in at the tint, gamma, and the values squeezed into the floor-to-ceiling range; then WebP (method 6, sharp YUV, no metadata) at the highest quality within 300,000 bytes. Every picture fits at quality 92. The Processing column lists the kind, the crop and the softening, then the level's own changes to its stage's look.

## Audio

Teggle ships no audio files. Every sound effect and the Fever music are synthesized in the browser at run time with the Web Audio API: oscillators, generated white noise, filters, envelopes, FM and a generated room impulse for the reverb. No recorded samples, sound libraries, MIDI files or recordings are used. A few heavy sounds and the music are rendered once into memory with an OfflineAudioContext after audio starts; that is still the same synthesis code.

| File | What it makes |
| --- | --- |
| `src/game/audio/synth.ts` | The instruments and building blocks: chimes, celesta, glockenspiel, timpani, drums, cymbals, brass, strings, bass, reed, noise hits, the room impulse and the master mix with its limiter. |
| `src/game/audio/sounds.ts` | Every sound effect (peg hits and pops, cannon, bucket, free balls, coin, style shots, warnings, HUD and UI sounds, the Fever sounds, every power and power moment). |
| `src/game/audio/score.ts` | "Ode to Joy", the finale theme of Beethoven's Symphony No. 9 (1824), public domain: the melody and harmony written out as note data in this file, and Teggle's arrangement of it. |
| `src/game/audio/music.ts` | Plays that arrangement on the instruments in `synth.ts`. |

The original game's sound files (in the owner's copy, outside the repository) were only measured, for length, pitch, envelope and brightness, so Teggle's sounds have a similar character (visual guide 14). None of their audio is copied, resampled, embedded or shipped. Review renders of every Teggle sound are made with `npm run audio:render` into `reference/teggle-audio/`, which is not committed.

## The original's levels

The one place Teggle ships original game art, at Tim's request on 2026-10-20 (spec 14.1, "The original's levels"): `public/original/` holds the original game's 55 campaign levels for Quick Play's Original section, written by `npm run original:import` from the owner's copy (`reference/peggle-2007/`, outside the repository). Source: the level files `levels/<name>.dat` (as dumped to `reference/peggle-2007/levels-json/`), the display names in `levels/stages.cfg`, and the pictures `levels/<name>.jp2`, all from the game's `main.pak`. `public/original/backgrounds/<name>.webp` is each picture as the game shows it: decoded with OpenJPEG `opj_decompress`, the two 800 x 600 ones (`3d`, `whirlpool`) cropped to the 646 x 543 the frame leaves visible, otherwise unchanged, then encoded by cwebp 1.6.0 at quality 92 (method 6, sharp YUV, no metadata): 2.5 MB for the 55.
